Let me start with a simple idea. You don't get what you want. You get what you become. [pause 2]

Now, why does that matter for learning? Because most people treat learning like an event. They read a book, they feel inspired for a weekend, and on Monday, nothing has changed.

Let me share three ideas with you today.

# Idea one: learning has seasons.

Think about a farmer. In the spring, he plants. He doesn't dig up the seeds on Tuesday to see if they're growing. He waters, he waits, he works.

Learning is the same. The first weeks feel like nothing is happening. That's not failure. That's spring.

Here's the key. Don't judge the harvest by the first week of planting. [pause 2]

So this week, pick one subject and give it a full season. Not a weekend. A season.

# Idea two: small and steady beats big and rare.

Twenty minutes a day will carry you further than a whole Saturday once a month. Why? Because the mind keeps what it visits often.

Here's a little phrase for you. Easy to do, easy not to do. [pause 2]

Reading ten pages is easy. Skipping ten pages is just as easy. And the difference, over a year, is everything.

# Idea three: teach it to keep it.

When you learn something, tell someone. Write it down in your own words. Explain it at dinner.

The moment you teach it, you find out whether you really know it. [pause 2]

---

So let's put it together. Learning has seasons, so be patient. Small and steady beats big and rare. And teach it to keep it. [pause 2]

Here's your one thing for this week. Choose your subject, set your twenty minutes, and tell one person what you learned by Sunday.

The seeds you plant this week are the harvest you live in next year.
